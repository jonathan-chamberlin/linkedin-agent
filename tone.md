# Post Tone & Style

Conversational and detached. First person when reflecting. Sound like someone learning, not an expert.

Target audience: recruiters and senior engineers. Goal: they see the post and think "this guy is getting better fast, he's someone I'd want to talk to."

## Voice
- Use contractions freely (I'm, it's, don't, won't)
- Short direct sentences, vary length within paragraphs
- Casual fillers are fine: "honestly," "basically," "really"
- Plain words over jargon, active verbs over abstract ones
- Keep personal examples brief

## Sentance Structure
- Lead with effect, follow with cause
- Combine related ideas into flowing sentences, don't break them into punchy fragments
- No "First... Second... Third..." lists. Merge into natural flow
- Vary comma-list length between 2 and 4 items
- Paragraph break when shifting tone (praise to critique, claim to response)

## What to Avoid
- No em dashes, no bolding/italics
- No hollow openers ("In today's world...", "I'm excited to share...")
- No "It's not X, it's Y" or "No X. No Y. Just Z." patterns
- No stacked "could" conditionals
- No closing rhetorical questions or "big picture" summary sentences
- No metaphors for experience. Use literal verbs (pretending, hiding) not figurative (dressed up as, masking)
- Don't explain why a technical choice worked. Just state what you did
- Don't announce what you're about to say. Just say it
- Don't use a term then immediately define it in the same sentence
- Cut hedging words: "probably," "arguably," "exactly"
- Cut redundant elaboration when context already makes the point

## Hook & Structure
- Open with the result or the surprise, not the setup. "I automated my LinkedIn posting with Playwright and Claude" not "I've been learning about browser automation"
- Show the delta, not the position. "Last week I didn't know what X was. Today I built Y with it." Speed of change is what's impressive, not current skill level
- Include one thing the reader can steal: a specific tool, technique, or realization. Not a tutorial, just a mention
- End with what you're building next, not a reflection. "Next I'm adding scheduled posts from a draft queue" not "I'm grateful for the journey"

## Credibility
- Acknowledge what you don't know
